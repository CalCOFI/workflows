The largest departure from normal in the CalCOFI record: what was measured, how it was checked and where it ranks

Author

CalCOFI

Published

2026-11-13

ImportantPreliminary data

The measurements from this cruise are preliminary: processed at sea, before final calibration (data_stage preliminary_with_bottle in release v2026.11.09). This analysis has not been peer reviewed. The checks under Is it real? are why the headline numbers are stated with confidence; their second decimal may change.

Summary

CalCOFI has measured the ocean off California at the same stations since 1949. Its fall 2026 cruise aboard R/V Sally Ride (31 October to 7 November 2026) found the largest departure from normal temperature in that record.

  • The measurement. On 31 October 2026, about 27 km off northern San Diego County (CalCOFI station 93.3 30.0), the water 40 to 50 m below the surface was 25.0 °C. The 1993–2013 November average at that station and depth is 12.2 °C.
  • The record. That departure, +12.8 °C, is the largest among the 581,071 station-and-depth values from 340 cruises (1950–2026) that have a baseline to compare with. The previous largest was +7.1 °C, on the same line in November 2015. The 1982–83 and 1997–98 El Niños peaked near +6 °C by the same measure.
  • At the surface the departure is smaller. The sea surface was 5 to 8 °C warmer than normal at the 17 stations of the inner Southern California Bight and 1 to 4 °C warmer at the 18 stations seaward of them. The largest numbers are below the surface because the warm layer is unusually thick: water of 25 °C reached 55 m, where a normal November is already near 12 °C.
  • Averaged over the area sampled. Through the upper 50 m at the 32 stations sampled, the ocean was 5.1 °C warmer than normal. The previous high over those same stations was +3.4 °C (November 2015). At the peak of the 1997–98 El Niño it was +2.1 °C.
  • It is different water. The warm layer is also unusually salty (34.43–34.48, about 1.0 above normal): the mark of water from the subtropics, not of local water warmed in place.
  • It has been checked. The instrument’s two temperature sensors agree within 0.01 °C through the warm layer, and its salinity matches water samples analysed on board. A buoy 2 km from one station read 25.1 °C when the instrument read 25.0 °C. The shore station on Scripps Pier recorded both the heat and the salt on its own instruments. Satellite analyses show the surface there about 5 °C above normal on the same day.
  • What it does not show. The cruise sampled 36 stations on CalCOFI’s three southern lines, off Southern California, and says nothing about waters farther north. The measurements show how warm the water is, not why.
TipDescribing it correctly
  • “12.8 °C warmer than normal” needs “at 40 to 50 m depth” beside it. At the sea surface the figure is 5 to 8 °C in the inner Southern California Bight.
  • For the region, the fair figure is the layer average: about 5 °C warmer than normal through the upper 50 m, against a previous record of 3.4 °C.
  • “Normal” is the 1993–2013 average for November at the same station and depth.
  • The data are preliminary.

1 What CalCOFI measures, and what “normal” means here

The California Cooperative Oceanic Fisheries Investigations (CalCOFI) has occupied a fixed grid of stations off California since 1949, four times a year in recent decades. At each station a CTD (an instrument that records conductivity, temperature and depth) is lowered to about 500 m, and water is collected in bottles at about 20 depths. The stations sit on numbered lines that run offshore from the coast: line 93.3 leaves the coast north of San Diego, line 90.0 at Dana Point and line 86.7 near Santa Monica Bay.

This page uses the CalCOFI Integrated Database, release v2026.11.09 (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24083894), in which two temperature series reach back through the record:

  • the CTD series (temperature_ave, the average of the instrument’s two sensors), 1993–2026, with a value every 10 m;
  • the bottle series (temperature), 1949–2021, at the bottle depths.

Normal is the release’s own climatology table: for each station, each 10 m depth bin and each calendar month, the mean of everything measured there on cruises of that month during 1993–2013, kept only where at least five cruises contribute. A departure (an anomaly) is a measurement minus the normal of its own station, depth and month. It is the same baseline the Explorer and the CTD transects subtract. Where no normal exists a departure is left blank; it is never treated as zero.

The fall cruise does not sail in the same month every year, and that matters for what follows. “November” on this page means the cruises whose median cast date falls in November.

2.2 What kind of anomaly this is

Read the profiles before the numbers. At stations 26.7, 28 and 30 the cruise found a mixed layer of about 25 °C reaching 55–57 m (by the 0.125 kg m⁻³ density criterion; the November normal there is 15–17 m). Below it the temperature falls steeply. The departure peaks at 40–60 m because that is where a normal November has already dropped into the cold water of the thermocline. The sea surface was about 7 °C above normal; the thickness of the layer adds the rest.

The water is also a different water mass. Salinity in the layer is 34.43–34.48 at stations 28 and 30, 0.94 to 1.16 above the November normal, where the normal’s own standard deviation is about 0.11. Warming the local surface layer does not change its salt. The water mass takes this up below.

Salinity is measured twice by unrelated methods: the CTD computes it from conductivity and temperature, and a salinometer measures it on the bottled water samples in the ship’s laboratory. In the warm layer the two agree to within 0.002. A temperature error of only 0.1 °C would shift the CTD’s salinity by 0.08 and break that agreement; an error of several degrees is out of the question.

The oxygen titrated from the same bottles (4.79–4.89 mL/L down to 50 m) is what a mixed layer in contact with the air holds at 25 °C (saturation 4.75 mL/L). Water at the normal temperature of those depths would hold about 5.7 mL/L.

8 Methods

  • Data. CalCOFI Integrated Database release v2026.11.09, read through its catalog with calcofi4r (cc_catalog(), cc_release_sources()); every object is listed with its URL under Data sources. Tables: obs_env (the thinned CTD series and the bottle series, with the release’s quality predicate qual_ok), climatology, obs_ctd_full (one-metre CTD casts of the cruise), sample, sample_measurement, cruise.
  • A cell is one cruise × station × 10 m depth bin of one series: the mean of its quality-ok values.
  • A departure is a cell minus the climatology row of the same series, station, depth bin and measurement type for the cruise’s month (cruise.month_median, the month of its median cast). The seasonal departure uses the row’s season_mean (December–February, March–May, June–August, September–November).
  • A layer mean averages a station’s profile over every 10 m bin of the layer, after linear interpolation in depth between sampled bins (never across more than 60 m, never beyond the sampled range), and requires every bin. A cruise mean averages the stations.
  • The map’s shading is ordinary kriging by calcofi4r::cc_interpolate(), the interpolator of the Explorer’s contour maps, clipped to the hull of the stations (plus 15 km), masked over land and limited to the range of the measured values.
  • Mixed-layer depth is the release’s mld_sigma_theta_0125 (the depth where density exceeds its 10 m value by 0.125 kg m⁻³), with its own November normal.
  • Outside sources (buoys, the Scripps Pier shore station, satellite analyses, Argo) are fetched by scripts/temperature_anomaly_outside_sources.py and kept as small tables in data/2026-11_temperature-anomaly/, because the buoy files hold only the last 45 days.

CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE cell AS
-- every temperature cell in the record with its departure from the release's own climatology.
-- a CELL is one cruise x station (site_key) x 10 m depth bin of one dataset: the mean of its quality-ok observations.
-- its baseline is the climatology row of the SAME dataset, station, depth bin and measurement type for the CRUISE's
-- month (obs_env.cruise_month = cruise.month_median), the rule every CalCOFI app applies. A cell whose month has no
-- monthly baseline keeps anom NULL: never 0. The seasonal baseline (the cruise's season) rides along as anom_season.
WITH o AS (
  SELECT dataset_key, measurement_type, cruise_key, site_key, depth_bin,
         any_value(cruise_month) AS cruise_month, any_value(year) AS cruise_year,
         avg(value) AS v, count(*) AS n_obs, avg(depth_min_m) AS depth_m,
         avg(latitude) AS latitude, avg(longitude) AS longitude, min(datetime) AS datetime
  FROM obs_env
  WHERE measurement_type IN ('temperature_ave', 'temperature')          -- CTD sensor-pair average; bottle (QC'd)
    AND dataset_key IN ('calcofi_ctd-cast', 'calcofi_bottle')
    AND qual_ok AND value IS NOT NULL AND depth_bin IS NOT NULL AND site_key IS NOT NULL
  GROUP BY ALL)
SELECT o.*,
       TRY_CAST(split_part(o.site_key, ' ', 1) AS DOUBLE) AS line,
       TRY_CAST(split_part(o.site_key, ' ', 2) AS DOUBLE) AS station,
       c.clim_mean, c.clim_sd, c.clim_n, c.n_cruises, c.season_mean, c.season_sd, c.season_n_cruises,
       o.v - c.clim_mean   AS anom,
       o.v - c.season_mean AS anom_season
FROM o
LEFT JOIN climatology c
  ON c.dataset_key = o.dataset_key AND c.measurement_type = o.measurement_type AND c.site_key = o.site_key
 AND c.month = o.cruise_month AND c.depth_bin = o.depth_bin

CREATE OR REPLACE MACRO interp_profile(col) AS TABLE
-- an anomaly (or temperature) profile on every 10 m bin from 0 to 190 m: linear interpolation IN DEPTH between the
-- bins that were sampled (never across more than 60 m, never beyond the deepest or above the shallowest sampled bin,
-- never in time or between stations). It puts the bottle series (standard depths) on the CTD's footing.
WITH a AS (SELECT dataset_key, cruise_key, site_key, depth_bin, col AS a FROM cell WHERE col IS NOT NULL AND depth_bin < 260),
casts AS (SELECT DISTINCT dataset_key, cruise_key, site_key FROM a),
t AS (SELECT c.*, b.depth_bin FROM casts c, (SELECT unnest(range(0, 200, 10)) AS depth_bin) b)
SELECT t.dataset_key, t.cruise_key, t.site_key, t.depth_bin,
       CASE WHEN lo.depth_bin = hi.depth_bin THEN lo.a
            WHEN hi.depth_bin - lo.depth_bin <= 60 THEN lo.a + (hi.a - lo.a) * (t.depth_bin - lo.depth_bin) / (hi.depth_bin - lo.depth_bin) END AS a
FROM t
ASOF LEFT JOIN a lo ON lo.dataset_key = t.dataset_key AND lo.cruise_key = t.cruise_key AND lo.site_key = t.site_key AND lo.depth_bin <= t.depth_bin
ASOF LEFT JOIN a hi ON hi.dataset_key = t.dataset_key AND hi.cruise_key = t.cruise_key AND hi.site_key = t.site_key AND hi.depth_bin >= t.depth_bin

CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE station_layer AS
-- one row per cast x layer: the depth-mean where the layer is complete (every 10 m bin of it present)
WITH l AS (SELECT * FROM (VALUES ('surface (0-10 m)', 0, 0), ('0-50 m', 0, 40), ('50-200 m', 50, 190), ('0-200 m', 0, 190)) AS t(layer, b0, b1))
SELECT p.dataset_key, p.cruise_key, p.site_key, l.layer,
       TRY_CAST(split_part(p.site_key, ' ', 1) AS DOUBLE) AS line, TRY_CAST(split_part(p.site_key, ' ', 2) AS DOUBLE) AS station,
       CASE WHEN count(p.anom)        = (l.b1 - l.b0) / 10 + 1 THEN avg(p.anom) END        AS anom,
       CASE WHEN count(p.anom_season) = (l.b1 - l.b0) / 10 + 1 THEN avg(p.anom_season) END AS anom_season,
       CASE WHEN count(p.temp)        = (l.b1 - l.b0) / 10 + 1 THEN avg(p.temp) END        AS temp
FROM prof p JOIN l ON p.depth_bin BETWEEN l.b0 AND l.b1
GROUP BY p.dataset_key, p.cruise_key, p.site_key, l.layer, l.b0, l.b1

  under load, and a value it could not serve is left empty and filled by the next run. OUTSIDE_OFFLINE=1 rebuilds the

def fetch(url, cache_dir, name=None, tries=3, wait=4):
  """the body of a URL, from the cache when a previous run already has it; None when the server will not serve it"""
  f = os.path.join(cache_dir, name or hashlib.sha1(url.encode()).hexdigest()[:16] + ".txt")
  if os.path.exists(f) and os.path.getsize(f) > 0:
    SOURCES.append((url, datetime.fromtimestamp(os.path.getmtime(f), timezone.utc).strftime("%Y-%m-%dT%H:%MZ"), "ok"))
    return open(f, encoding="utf-8", errors="replace").read()
  if os.environ.get("OUTSIDE_OFFLINE"):                        # build the tables from the cache alone, asking no server
    return None
  err = None
  for i in range(tries):
    try:
      with urllib.request.urlopen(urllib.request.Request(url, headers=UA), timeout=45) as r:
        body = r.read()
      open(f, "wb").write(body)
      SOURCES.append((url, datetime.now(timezone.utc).strftime("%Y-%m-%dT%H:%MZ"), "ok"))
      return body.decode("utf-8", "replace")
    except Exception as e:
      err = e; time.sleep(wait * (i + 1))
  print(f"  not served: {url} ({err})", file=sys.stderr, flush=True)
  SOURCES.append((url, datetime.now(timezone.utc).strftime("%Y-%m-%dT%H:%MZ"), f"failed: {err}"))
  return None

def km(lat1, lon1, lat2, lon2):
  p1, p2 = math.radians(lat1), math.radians(lat2)
  a = math.sin((p2 - p1) / 2) ** 2 + math.cos(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.sin(math.radians(lon2 - lon1) / 2) ** 2
  return 6371.0 * 2 * math.asin(math.sqrt(a))

def write(path, rows, fields=None):
  with open(path, "w", newline="") as f:
    w = csv.DictWriter(f, fieldnames=fields or list(rows[0].keys())); w.writeheader(); w.writerows(rows)
  print(f"wrote {path} ({len(rows)} rows)", flush=True)

    return pts

    print("  ", sat[-1]["site_key"], sat[-1]["ctd_t_top3m"], sat[-1]["oisst_sst"], sat[-1]["oisst_anom"], sat[-1]["mur_sst"], sat[-1]["mur_anom"], flush=True)

  write(os.path.join(out, "sources.csv"), [dict(url=u, accessed_utc=t, status=s) for u, t, s in SOURCES])

CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE cell AS
-- every temperature cell in the record with its departure from the release's own climatology.
-- a CELL is one cruise x station (site_key) x 10 m depth bin of one dataset: the mean of its quality-ok observations.
-- its baseline is the climatology row of the SAME dataset, station, depth bin and measurement type for the CRUISE's
-- month (obs_env.cruise_month = cruise.month_median), the rule every CalCOFI app applies. A cell whose month has no
-- monthly baseline keeps anom NULL: never 0. The seasonal baseline (the cruise's season) rides along as anom_season.
WITH o AS (
  SELECT dataset_key, measurement_type, cruise_key, site_key, depth_bin,
         any_value(cruise_month) AS cruise_month, any_value(year) AS cruise_year,
         avg(value) AS v, count(*) AS n_obs, avg(depth_min_m) AS depth_m,
         avg(latitude) AS latitude, avg(longitude) AS longitude, min(datetime) AS datetime
  FROM obs_env
  WHERE measurement_type IN ('temperature_ave', 'temperature')          -- CTD sensor-pair average; bottle (QC'd)
    AND dataset_key IN ('calcofi_ctd-cast', 'calcofi_bottle')
    AND qual_ok AND value IS NOT NULL AND depth_bin IS NOT NULL AND site_key IS NOT NULL
  GROUP BY ALL)
SELECT o.*,
       TRY_CAST(split_part(o.site_key, ' ', 1) AS DOUBLE) AS line,
       TRY_CAST(split_part(o.site_key, ' ', 2) AS DOUBLE) AS station,
       c.clim_mean, c.clim_sd, c.clim_n, c.n_cruises, c.season_mean, c.season_sd, c.season_n_cruises,
       o.v - c.clim_mean   AS anom,
       o.v - c.season_mean AS anom_season
FROM o
LEFT JOIN climatology c
  ON c.dataset_key = o.dataset_key AND c.measurement_type = o.measurement_type AND c.site_key = o.site_key
 AND c.month = o.cruise_month AND c.depth_bin = o.depth_bin

CREATE OR REPLACE MACRO interp_profile(col) AS TABLE
-- an anomaly (or temperature) profile on every 10 m bin from 0 to 190 m: linear interpolation IN DEPTH between the
-- bins that were sampled (never across more than 60 m, never beyond the deepest or above the shallowest sampled bin,
-- never in time or between stations). It puts the bottle series (standard depths) on the CTD's footing.
WITH a AS (SELECT dataset_key, cruise_key, site_key, depth_bin, col AS a FROM cell WHERE col IS NOT NULL AND depth_bin < 260),
casts AS (SELECT DISTINCT dataset_key, cruise_key, site_key FROM a),
t AS (SELECT c.*, b.depth_bin FROM casts c, (SELECT unnest(range(0, 200, 10)) AS depth_bin) b)
SELECT t.dataset_key, t.cruise_key, t.site_key, t.depth_bin,
       CASE WHEN lo.depth_bin = hi.depth_bin THEN lo.a
            WHEN hi.depth_bin - lo.depth_bin <= 60 THEN lo.a + (hi.a - lo.a) * (t.depth_bin - lo.depth_bin) / (hi.depth_bin - lo.depth_bin) END AS a
FROM t
ASOF LEFT JOIN a lo ON lo.dataset_key = t.dataset_key AND lo.cruise_key = t.cruise_key AND lo.site_key = t.site_key AND lo.depth_bin <= t.depth_bin
ASOF LEFT JOIN a hi ON hi.dataset_key = t.dataset_key AND hi.cruise_key = t.cruise_key AND hi.site_key = t.site_key AND hi.depth_bin >= t.depth_bin

       TRY_CAST(split_part(p.site_key, ' ', 1) AS DOUBLE) AS line, TRY_CAST(split_part(p.site_key, ' ', 2) AS DOUBLE) AS station,
       CASE WHEN count(p.anom)        = (l.b1 - l.b0) / 10 + 1 THEN avg(p.anom) END        AS anom,
       CASE WHEN count(p.anom_season) = (l.b1 - l.b0) / 10 + 1 THEN avg(p.anom_season) END AS anom_season,
       CASE WHEN count(p.temp)        = (l.b1 - l.b0) / 10 + 1 THEN avg(p.temp) END        AS temp

GROUP BY p.dataset_key, p.cruise_key, p.site_key, l.layer, l.b0, l.b1

## Summary {.unnumbered}

CalCOFI has measured the ocean off California at the same stations since 1949. Its fall 2026 cruise aboard
R/V *Sally Ride* (`r format(cr$date_min, "%e %B") |> trimws()` to `r format(cr$date_max, "%e %B %Y") |> trimws()`)
found the largest departure from normal temperature in that record.

- **The measurement.** On `r format(k$top_date, "%e %B %Y") |> trimws()`, about `r k$top_km` km off northern San
  Diego County (CalCOFI station 93.3 30.0), the water 40 to 50 m below the surface was **`r f1(k$top_v)` °C**.
  The 1993–2013 November average at that station and depth is **`r f1(k$top_clim)` °C**.
- **The record.** That departure, **`r fs(k$top_anom)` °C**, is the largest among the `r fk(d_share$n_cells)`
  station-and-depth values from `r d_share$n_cruises` cruises (`r d_share$yr0`–2026) that have a baseline to
  compare with. The previous largest was `r fs(k$prev_anom)` °C, on the same line in November 2015. The 1982–83 and
  1997–98 El Niños peaked near +6 °C by the same measure.
- **At the surface the departure is smaller.** The sea surface was `r f1(min(inner$anom), 0)` to
  `r f1(max(inner$anom), 0)` °C warmer than normal at the `r nrow(inner)` stations of the inner Southern California
  Bight and `r f1(min(outer$anom), 0)` to `r f1(max(outer$anom), 0)` °C warmer at the `r nrow(outer)` stations
  seaward of them. The largest numbers are below the surface because the warm layer is unusually thick: water of
  25 °C reached 55 m, where a normal November is already near 12 °C.
- **Averaged over the area sampled.** Through the upper 50 m at the `r k$l50_n` stations sampled, the ocean was
  **`r f1(k$l50)` °C warmer than normal**. The previous high over those same stations was
  `r fs(k$l50_prev$anom)` °C (November 2015). At the peak of the 1997–98 El Niño it was `r fs(k$feb98)` °C.
- **It is different water.** The warm layer is also unusually salty (`r f1(k$sal[1], 2)`–`r f1(k$sal[2], 2)`, about
  `r f1(mean(k$sal_anom))` above normal): the mark of water from the subtropics, not of local water warmed in place.
- **It has been checked.** The instrument's two temperature sensors agree within 0.01 °C through the warm layer,
  and its salinity matches water samples analysed on board. A buoy 2 km from one station read 25.1 °C when the
  instrument read 25.0 °C. The shore station on Scripps Pier recorded both the heat and the salt on its own
  instruments. Satellite analyses show the surface there about 5 °C above normal on the same day.
- **What it does not show.** The cruise sampled `r n_st` stations on CalCOFI's three southern lines, off Southern
  California, and says nothing about waters farther north. The measurements show how warm the water is, not why.

::: {.callout-tip title="Describing it correctly"}
- "`r f1(k$top_anom)` °C warmer than normal" needs "**at 40 to 50 m depth**" beside it. At the sea surface the
  figure is `r f1(min(inner$anom), 0)` to `r f1(max(inner$anom), 0)` °C in the inner Southern California Bight.
- For the region, the fair figure is the layer average: **about `r f1(k$l50, 0)` °C warmer than normal through the
  upper 50 m**, against a previous record of `r f1(k$l50_prev$anom)` °C.
- "Normal" is the 1993–2013 average for November at the same station and depth.
- The data are preliminary.
:::

## What CalCOFI measures, and what "normal" means here {#sec-background}

The California Cooperative Oceanic Fisheries Investigations (CalCOFI) has occupied a fixed grid of stations off
California since 1949, four times a year in recent decades. At each station a CTD (an instrument that records
conductivity, temperature and depth) is lowered to about 500 m, and water is collected in bottles at about 20
depths. The stations sit on numbered lines that run offshore from the coast: line 93.3 leaves the coast north of
San Diego, line 90.0 at Dana Point and line 86.7 near Santa Monica Bay.

This page uses the CalCOFI Integrated Database, release **`r VER`** (<https://doi.org/`r CAT$doi`>), in which two
temperature series reach back through the record:

- the **CTD** series (`temperature_ave`, the average of the instrument's two sensors), `r q("SELECT min(cruise_year) AS y FROM cell WHERE dataset_key = 'calcofi_ctd-cast'")$y`–2026, with a value every 10 m;
- the **bottle** series (`temperature`), `r q("SELECT min(cruise_year) AS y FROM cell WHERE dataset_key = 'calcofi_bottle'")$y`–`r q("SELECT max(cruise_year) AS y FROM cell WHERE dataset_key = 'calcofi_bottle'")$y`, at the bottle depths.

**Normal** is the release's own `climatology` table: for each station, each 10 m depth bin and each calendar month,
the mean of everything measured there on cruises of that month during **1993–2013**, kept only where at least five
cruises contribute. A **departure** (an anomaly) is a measurement minus the normal of its own station, depth and
month. It is the same baseline the [Explorer](https://calcofi.io/explore/) and the
[CTD transects](https://calcofi.io/ctd-transects/?line=93.3&cruise=2026-11-33P4&var=temperature_ave&mode=anomaly)
subtract. Where no normal exists a departure is left blank; it is never treated as zero.

The fall cruise does not sail in the same month every year, and that matters for what follows. "November" on this
page means the cruises whose median cast date falls in November.

### What kind of anomaly this is

Read the profiles before the numbers. At stations 26.7, 28 and 30 the cruise found a **mixed layer of about 25 °C
reaching `r f1(min(k$mld), 0)`–`r f1(max(k$mld), 0)` m** (by the 0.125 kg m⁻³ density criterion; the November normal
there is `r f1(min(k$mld_clim), 0)`–`r f1(max(k$mld_clim), 0)` m). Below it the temperature falls steeply. The
departure peaks at 40–60 m because that is where a normal November has already dropped into the cold water of the
thermocline. The sea surface was about 7 °C above normal; the thickness of the layer adds the rest.

The water is also a different water mass. Salinity in the layer is **`r f1(k$sal[1], 2)`–`r f1(k$sal[2], 2)`** at
stations 28 and 30, `r f1(k$sal_anom[1], 2)` to `r f1(k$sal_anom[2], 2)` above the November normal, where the
normal's own standard deviation is about `r f1(mean(d_sal$sd), 2)`. Warming the local surface layer does not change
its salt. [The water mass](#sec-watermass) takes this up below.

The CTD carries two independent temperature sensors. Through the warm layer at stations 28 and 30 (the top 50 m,
`r chk$pair_core$n` one-metre values) they never differ by more than **`r f1(chk$pair_core$max, 3)` °C**; half of
the values differ by `r f1(chk$pair_core$median * 1000, 1)` thousandths of a degree or less. Over every cast of the

Each station is also measured twice, on the way down and on the way up. At those two stations the two passes
through the top 40 m are `r chk$gap[1]` to `r chk$gap[2]` minutes apart and differ by `r f1(chk$du_core$mean, 2)` °C

Salinity is measured twice by unrelated methods: the CTD computes it from conductivity **and temperature**, and a
salinometer measures it on the bottled water samples in the ship's laboratory. In the warm layer the two agree to

temperature of those depths would hold about `r f1(btl$o2_nrm, 1)` mL/L.

### The thinned series against the full profile

for the full one-metre profile.

1994 value is `r f1(tail(c94$v, 1), 2)` °C in the last bin of a cast that reads `r f1(c94$v[nrow(c94) - 1], 2)` °C
ten metres above it. The October 1959 cast at station 93.3 35.0 falls in a perfectly straight line from
`r f1(c59$v[1], 1)` °C at `r round(c59$depth_m[1])` m to `r f1(tail(c59$v, 1), 1)` °C at `r round(tail(c59$depth_m, 1))` m
(`r f1(abs(mean(s59)) * 25, 2)` °C every 25 m):
values interpolated across a gap in the source, not measurements. Neither is flagged in the source data. Without
them the largest departure before 1993 is `r fs(pre93$anom, 2)` °C (`r format(pre93$date, "%B %Y")`).

Temperature alone cannot say where water came from; temperature and salinity together can. The seaward stations
plot where the California Current always does: fresh at the surface (`r f1(wm$s_outer[1], 1)`–`r f1(wm$s_outer[2], 1)`
in the top 40 m) and saltier with depth. The normal profile at the record station does the same
(`r f1(wm$s_norm[1], 2)`–`r f1(wm$s_norm[2], 2)` in the top 40 m, `r f1(wm$s_norm_max, 2)` at 200 m). Across the inner
Bight the top 40 m is `r f1(wm$s_inner[1], 1)` to `r f1(wm$s_inner[2], 1)`, and at stations 28 and 30 the warm layer
(`r f1(k$sal[1], 2)`–`r f1(k$sal[2], 2)`) is saltier than any water the normal column holds down to 200 m, while
lying at the surface, where the normal is freshest. Local heating moves water straight up on this diagram; it cannot
move it to the right.

The quantity of heat makes the same point. Through the upper 200 m at station `r hc_top$site_key` the water column
holds about **`r f1(hc_top$gj)` GJ per square metre** more heat than normal
(`r f1(mean(hc$gj))` GJ m⁻² on average over the `r nrow(hc)` stations sampled to 200 m). At a net surface heating of
100 W m⁻², a generous figure chosen only for scale, the atmosphere would need about `r fk(round(wm$days, -1))` days to
supply it. Both the salt and the heat say this water was carried in, not warmed where it lies. The
[Scripps Pier record](#fig-pier) shows it arriving at the coast: salinity there rose by `r f1(p_pk$s26 - p_c0$s26, 1)`
within about ten days.

```{r}
#| label: tbl-coverage
d_cov <- q("
  SELECT CASE dataset_key WHEN 'calcofi_bottle' THEN 'bottle' ELSE 'CTD' END AS series, CAST((cruise_year // 10) * 10 AS INTEGER) || 's' AS decade,

         count(DISTINCT cruise_key) FILTER (WHERE anom_season IS NOT NULL) AS with_seasonal_normal,

  FROM cell GROUP BY ALL ORDER BY series DESC, decade")
n_site <- q("SELECT count(DISTINCT site_key) AS n, min(line) AS l0, max(line) AS l1 FROM cell WHERE anom IS NOT NULL")
months <- q("SELECT DISTINCT cruise_month AS m FROM cell WHERE anom IS NOT NULL ORDER BY 1")$m
kable(d_cov, col.names = c("series", "decade", "cruises", "with a monthly normal", "with a seasonal normal", "% of values with a monthly normal"),
      caption = "How much of the record has a normal to compare with.")
```

- **"The record" is the part of the record with a baseline.** A normal exists for `r n_site$n` stations of the
  present grid (lines `r n_site$l0` to `r n_site$l1`) and for the months with at least five cruises in 1993–2013:
  `r paste(month.name[months], collapse = ", ")`. Earlier decades sampled other months and other stations far more
  often. "The largest on record" means the largest among `r d_share$n_cruises` of `r n_cruises_all` cruises.
  The baseline-free comparisons ([shared stations](#tbl-paired), [record temperatures](#fig-warmest)) do not have
  this limit and give the same answer.
- **The baseline is thin.** `r n_base[1]` to `r n_base[2]` November cruises per bin on line 93.3. The
  [baseline table](#tbl-baseline) bounds what that can change: the peak departure stays between
  `r f1(base_rng[1])` and `r f1(base_rng[2])` °C.
- **Coverage.** `r n_st` stations in `r as.integer(cr$date_max - cr$date_min) + 1` days, where a full cruise
  occupies 65 to 75. The layer averages are over those stations only; a whole-grid average would be lower and
  cannot be computed for this cruise.
- **Preliminary.** The casts are one-metre averages processed at sea. Two sensors agreeing within
  `r f1(chk$pair_core$max, 3)` °C and bottle salinities agreeing within `r f1(btl$ds, 3)` leave little for final
  calibration to change.
- **Single values or layer averages.** A ranking of single values rewards the thermocline, where a displaced layer
  makes the largest numbers, and it is where bad values in old data surface. The layer average over fixed stations
  is the fairer claim. Both rank fall 2026 first.
- **Cause.** These measurements describe the water. They do not attribute it.

## Methods {#sec-methods}

- **Data.** CalCOFI Integrated Database release `r VER`, read through its catalog with
  [`calcofi4r`](https://calcofi.io/calcofi4r/) (`cc_catalog()`, `cc_release_sources()`); every object is listed
  with its URL under [Data sources](#sec-data). Tables: `obs_env` (the thinned CTD series and the bottle series,
  with the release's quality predicate `qual_ok`), `climatology`, `obs_ctd_full` (one-metre CTD casts of the
  cruise), `sample`, `sample_measurement`, `cruise`.
- **A cell** is one cruise × station × 10 m depth bin of one series: the mean of its quality-ok values.
- **A departure** is a cell minus the `climatology` row of the same series, station, depth bin and measurement
  type for the cruise's month (`cruise.month_median`, the month of its median cast). The seasonal departure uses the
  row's `season_mean` (December–February, March–May, June–August, September–November).
- **A layer mean** averages a station's profile over every 10 m bin of the layer, after linear interpolation in
  depth between sampled bins (never across more than 60 m, never beyond the sampled range), and requires every bin.
  A cruise mean averages the stations.
- **The map's shading** is ordinary kriging by `calcofi4r::cc_interpolate()`, the interpolator of the Explorer's
  contour maps, clipped to the hull of the stations (plus 15 km), masked over land and limited to the range of the
  measured values.
- **Mixed-layer depth** is the release's `mld_sigma_theta_0125` (the depth where density exceeds its 10 m value by
  0.125 kg m⁻³), with its own November normal.
- **Outside sources** (buoys, the Scripps Pier shore station, satellite analyses, Argo) are fetched by
  `scripts/temperature_anomaly_outside_sources.py` and kept as small tables in
  `data/2026-11_temperature-anomaly/`, because the buoy files hold only the last 45 days.
