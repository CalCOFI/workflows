1 What changed

Erin, 2026-10-02 (email “Stations (and lines?) overlaid on WEAs”): “I need a map of the CalCOFI stations (and lines, if possible) with the Wind Energy Areas behind them (and maybe even gas and oil areas and AOAs).”

The CalCOFI Explorer now draws its energy and industry boundaries from the Ocean Metrics places gazetteer, so the other apps can use the same layers. Six layers are wired in, under Layers → Add a layer → Energy & Industry:

The Explorer also has a new Observations layer (Section 5): every cast at its own position as a black dot, drawn with or without the data, so the maps below show the sampling over the sea floor and the boundaries with no temperature colour.

Every map below is a link: it opens the same view, and everything on it (lens, layers, colours, extent) can be changed from there. The screenshots are from release v2026.10.08.

2 Every cast over the wind areas, sea floor only

Each black dot is a site where temperature was sampled (a CTD or bottle cast, at its own position to 0.01°, all years, 0–500 m), over the GEBCO sea floor and the six layers, with the data layer off. The repeat occupations of a station make the clusters; the grid of clusters is the station pattern, and its diagonal rows are the lines.

Open in the Explorer: Point Conception to San Diego

Off the Central Coast, the Morro Bay leases (purple) sit over the rescinded Morro Bay WEA (brown), with the oil and gas leases off Point Arguello to the south. Hover a lease for its status.

5 How to draw it yourself

Open Layers (top right of the map): untick Data, tick Observations and pick its colour, then add or remove boundary layers under Add a layer → Energy & Industry. The dots follow the selection, so choosing another variable, an organism, years, seasons or depths changes which casts or tows are drawn. The link (Share, or the address bar) carries all of it: data=off&obs=on.

If a single dot per station is wanted instead, the Stations lens with the data on draws one, here shaded by its count of observations (open in the Explorer).

6 How many stations go into a surface

Erin, 2026-10-02: “To be able to see how many stations are actually going into the interpolation/mean for a given summarized area.”

In the Contours lens, fitted to the station grid, show the inputs draws every station the surface was fitted to, sized by how many observations it holds, and the checkbox gives their number (222 stations here). Under it the fit reports itself: the number of points, the leave-one-out error and the variogram. The Stations lens with count of observations (Figure 7) gives the same at each station, and Regions gives a mean and its count inside each polygon of a boundary layer (sanctuaries, protected areas, counties; not yet the gazetteer layers above).

Open in the Explorer: Contours over the station grid, inputs shown

7 The lines

On the 2-D map the lines read as the diagonal rows of clusters (Figure 1), but they are not labelled there yet. The Sections lens in 3-D draws the whole station pattern at the sea surface, each line threaded and named at both ends, and every station numbered.

Open in the Explorer: line 80 in 3-D

8 Diablo Canyon and the age of the wind data

Erin, 2026-10-05: “I think that the Diablo canyon area is no longer a thing right? Just verifying that the WEA dataset is a recent one.” Answered by email on 2026-10-08:

  • Diablo Canyon was only a 2018 call area. It was never designated a WEA or leased, and it is in none of BOEM’s current layers.
  • BOEM rescinded all designated WEAs, including Humboldt and Morro Bay, on 30 July 2025. They are the rescinded history layer (Table 1).
  • The five California leases from the December 2022 auction are still drawn, each with its status from BOEM’s California page in the popup (cancelled, relinquished, under settlement or unchanged).
  • The Data Basin layer is a 2022 snapshot of the proposed, pre-auction lease areas, so it is not used.

The gazetteer re-checks its sources weekly and records when each layer was retrieved (Table 1).

## What changed {#sec-changed}

Erin, 2026-10-02 (email "Stations (and lines?) overlaid on WEAs"): *"I need a map of the CalCOFI

The [CalCOFI Explorer](https://calcofi.io/explore/) now draws its energy and industry boundaries
from the [Ocean Metrics places gazetteer](https://oceanmetrics.io/places), so the other apps can
use the same layers. Six layers are wired in, under **Layers → Add a layer → Energy & Industry**:

| Explorer layer | What it is | Gazetteer collection |
|---|---|---|
| BOEM Wind Leases | lease outlines; the popup gives each lease's current status and status date | [`boem_wind_leases`](https://storage.oceanmetrics.io/gazetteer/boem_wind_leases/README.md) |
| BOEM Wind Planning Areas (rescinded) | the planning areas and WEAs BOEM rescinded on 30 July 2025, kept as history | [`boem_wind_planning_rescinded`](https://storage.oceanmetrics.io/gazetteer/boem_wind_planning_rescinded/README.md) |
| BOEM OCS Planning Areas | Outer Continental Shelf oil and gas planning areas | [`boem_ocs_planning`](https://storage.oceanmetrics.io/gazetteer/boem_ocs_planning/README.md) |
| BOEM 11th National OCS Program (Draft Proposed) | the draft program areas, including those off California | [`boem_program_11_draft`](https://storage.oceanmetrics.io/gazetteer/boem_program_11_draft/README.md) |
| BOEM Pacific Oil & Gas Leases | active Pacific OCS oil and gas leases | [`boem_pacific_og_leases`](https://storage.oceanmetrics.io/gazetteer/boem_pacific_og_leases/README.md) |
| NOAA SoCal Aquaculture Opportunity Areas | NOAA Fisheries' Southern California AOAs | [`noaa_aoa_socal`](https://storage.oceanmetrics.io/gazetteer/noaa_aoa_socal/README.md) |
