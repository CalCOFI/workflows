A face, a size and a sentence for every species page

The 2,410 pages under calcofi.io/species/ read the release faithfully and say nothing about the organism. This page tries the ways to fix that systematically: a silhouette, a licensed photo or plate, a length on a scale a reader already knows, and one sentence, each fetched by the taxon's own ids and shipped with its credit.

Every asset here was fetched today for the ten taxa in the cast bar, one from each world the database covers (a sardine and an anchovy, a hake, a lanternfish, a krill, a squid, a seabird, a dolphin, a diatom genus and the rockfish genus). Pick one to see how each idea holds up when the species has no length on record, no clean photo, or only a family-level silhouette. The recommendation is the composed head just below; the sections after it are the parts and their alternatives, then the sources with what each actually gives, and the build.

Recommendation

The page head, composed

The live head, unchanged, gains a face row: the PhyloPic silhouette in the page's own ink beside a licensed photo that fades into the band, a two-bar glance at size against a familiar object, and a sentence stitched from Wikipedia, the release record and WoRMS, each part underlined in the colour of its source. Light and dark are drawn together because the fade and the ink have to work on both.

Part 1

The face: silhouette, photo or plate

Three kinds of picture, three coverage stories. The silhouette is the only one every page can have, so it is the constant; the photo and the plate are enrichments that appear when a clean one exists and are simply absent when none does, without changing the layout.

Part 2

The treatment: one frame, one fade, three inks

Photos from four sources will never match in crop, light or background. What can match is the frame: a fixed 3 : 2 crop, and a radial mask that dissolves the edges into whatever ground the page has, so the messy corners of a Commons upload disappear and the same photo sits quietly on sand or on navy. The ink is the second dial: full colour keeps the field marks, muted colour calms a busy photo, and the brand-blue luminosity blend turns any photo into one house style at the cost of colour.

Part 3

How big is it

One logarithmic ladder from a hair to the ship, with CalCOFI's own gear on it: the 505 µm bongo mesh and the 71 cm ring say at a glance why a diatom is counted from a bottle and a krill from a net. The taxon's lengths sit under the axis: its adult maximum from WoRMS or SeaLifeBase and, for the fishes, the egg, hatching, flexion and transformation lengths FishBase carries from the NOAA and CalCOFI larval guides, which is where the ichthyoplankton and CUFES datasets actually meet the animal.

Beside something familiar

The nearest reference within an order of magnitude, drawn at the same scale as the silhouette. A person for a dolphin, the bongo ring for a sardine, a quarter for a krill.

What the nets and pumps catch

For the fishes, NOAA's Ichthyoplankton Information System serves a developmental plate, egg to late larva, from a 1989 NOAA technical report that is public domain as a US Government work. The lengths beside it are FishBase's, citing the same guides.

Part 4

The sentence

Prose is authored, facts are generated, and here the prose is borrowed: the first sentence or two of the Wikipedia article, under CC BY-SA with a link to the revision. The measured half is the release record's own numbers written as a sentence, and the authority line is WoRMS. Where Wikipedia only has the genus, the page says so rather than pretending.

How it would be built

One sidecar, fetched by id, shipped with its credit

The catalog's rule holds: nothing typed. A fetcher in the landing repo walks taxa.json, asks each source by the taxon's ids, keeps only what passes the licence policy, caches the pictures at 800 px on the public bucket, and writes one taxa_media.json. The Jekyll generator merges it by taxon_key, so a page whose taxon has no media renders exactly as it does today.

  1. taxa.jsonthe release record: 2,410 taxa with their WoRMS, ITIS and GBIF ids and lineage
  2. scripts/fetch_species_media.pyweekly in refresh.yml and after a release; PhyloPic → Wikidata → Commons → iNaturalist → GBIF → WoRMS → FishBase → NOAA IIS, keyed by id, rate-limited, resumable
  3. taxa_media.json + thumbson gs://calcofi-files-public/species-media/: one JSON, an 800 px WebP per picture, the source URL and licence beside each
  4. scripts/fetch_release.shpulls it into _data/ like the other release files; never committed
  5. _plugins/species.rbmerges by taxon_key; _includes/species_face.html draws the row; JSON-LD gains an ImageObject with license and creditText
  6. scripts/check_species_media.pyevery shown asset has an allow-listed licence, a credit, a live source URL and a taxon within two ranks

Licence policy

  • Take: CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA. Share-alike is fine: the cropped, faded copy is shared under the same terms.
  • Take, rank last, say so: CC BY-NC and CC BY-NC-SA. calcofi.io is non-commercial, but a downstream reuser is not, so the caption carries the licence.
  • Leave: any ND (the crop and the fade are derivatives) and anything without a licence field. iNaturalist's null means all rights reserved.
  • Never: Macaulay, FishBase and WoRMS photos, or Hornady's drawings, until someone says yes in writing.

Ranking, per slot

  • Taxon first: the page's own taxon, then a species under it (a genus page shows one and names it), then an ancestor within two ranks, flagged "stands in".
  • Curated over raw: Commons P18 and iNaturalist's taxon photos before the first research-grade observation; the developmental plate for larvae.
  • Then licence order, then size: CC0, PD, BY, BY-SA, NC; landscape, at least 800 px on the long side.
  • Focal point kept: a stored [x, y] so the 3 : 2 crop does not behead the fish.

Sizes

  • WoRMS body-size attributes first (they are FishBase's, checked), then SeaLifeBase, then Wikidata P2043. The kind of length (SL, TL, mantle) is kept and shown.
  • FishBase EGGS and LARVAE for egg diameter and the hatching, flexion and transformation lengths, citing their references.
  • The ladder's references live in a size_reference.csv with their sources; the mesh and ring are CalCOFI's own numbers.
  • No number is invented for a taxon without one; the glance says "not on record", and a curated size-class file can fill the phytoplankton later.

Found on the way

Notes and decisions

Findings

  • CUFES rows include zero counts. 75.5 % of the CUFES rows in obs_bio are zeros (38,929 of the sardine's 49,572), and every CUFES species carries the same 49,572 "observations" with the same 1998 peak. The species page says "obs"; the sentence here says "records". A per-dataset n_present beside n_obs in taxa.json would let both be honest.
  • PhyloPic's WoRMS ids are sparse; its names are not. Four of twelve resolved by AphiaID, all twelve by name with a walk up the lineage. The fetcher should try the id, then the name at each rank.
  • GBIF's licence filter is per dataset. Asking for CC0 and CC BY returned NC-ND images; filter on media[].license.
  • The first raw photo is rarely the portrait. Research-grade iNaturalist photos of the sardine, hake and rockfish were a fish on sand, a head on a rock and a catch in a hand; the curated Commons and iNat taxon photos were the ones to use.
  • Wikidata's P18 can be the sister species. The sardine's image on Wikidata is Sardinops melanostictus; the caption must name what is shown.
  • The lengths already cite the CalCOFI guides. FishBase's larval lengths reference Matarese et al. 1989 and the Atlas 33 chapters, so the numbers can be shown before any drawing is extracted.

For Ben to decide

  • Silhouette as the constant, photo as the enrichment (recommended), or photo-first with the silhouette as a fallback? The first keeps every page consistent and theme-safe; the second is warmer but 30 % of pages would fall back anyway.
  • Allow NC photos? Recommended yes, ranked last and labelled; the alternative leaves most plankton without a photo.
  • Which ink for photos: full colour with the fade (recommended: the field marks survive), muted, or the brand-blue luminosity blend?
  • The references on the ladder: the mesh and the ring are the CalCOFI-specific choice; the quarter and the person are the universal ones; the ship is the flourish. Keep all six or drop the ship?
  • The courtesy asks: AFSC for the plates (public domain, but they maintain the site), NOAA Fisheries for the Hornady drawings, SWFSC for Atlas 33.
