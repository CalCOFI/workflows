0 · What the page says today

Linear, and in words

The hero is a paragraph. The ship, the water and the years appear only as a line of monospace: ship → workflows → database → datasets → apps. Then five bands of the same shape.

Everything below the hero is good and stays — the catalog is the strongest thing on the site, and the product cards are honest screenshots. The gap is the first screen: it argues the pipeline instead of showing the ocean, so a first-time visitor reads about breadth rather than seeing it. Three moves fix that without touching the catalog:

  • Draw the sampling, to scale. One CalCOFI line as a section — the ship on the surface, each instrument hanging to its real depth, each kind of life at the depth it lives. It is the same picture CalCOFI has drawn of itself since the first atlases, and it is the Explorer's Sections lens.
  • Measure the reach. The station grid from the release's own grid table and a dataset-by-year strip from the coverage the release measures. Real numbers beside a real map.
  • Break the rhythm. A bento of ways in — where, when, what's new, explore, get the data — before the catalog, instead of five identical bands.

Every hotspot in the drawing links to calcofi.org's own method page (bottle, CTD, DIC, ichthyoplankton, zooplankton, seabirds, marine mammals, underway, NCOG), so calcofi.io never has to explain a bongo net — the MBON pattern, with the depth pushed to the program site.

1 · The hero

One line, from the coast to the deep

Line 90 as a section: the ship, five kinds of gear at their true depths, thirteen kinds of measurement at the depth they live. Hover a pin. The rosette is lowering.

Real: station spacing on Line 90, the 14 standard bottle depths, 515 m CTD, 210 m bongo, 70 m PairoVET, 3 m CUFES, the 13 categories and their glyphs from brand/v2/icons, every link a calcofi.org method page Placeholder: the ship, the sea floor profile (GEBCO along Line 90 in the real build), the organism art (part 5)

Why a section

It is the one picture that holds ship, instruments and species in a single frame with a true axis. Depth is the coordinate the whole database is bounded on; the horizontal axis is the CalCOFI line every cruise repeats. Nothing here is a metaphor.

Restraint

Thirteen pins, one to three words each; the sentence lives in the tooltip and the calcofi.org page behind it. No gears, no badges, no in-image text — the labels are HTML, so they translate, resize and theme.

Theme and motion

Every colour is a brand token, so the same drawing flips with ?theme=dark (try the button in the masthead: the sun becomes a moon, the water darkens). Two motions only — the rosette's cast and the CUFES intake — both off under reduced-motion.

What is measured here

The pin legend below is the category registry with the release's counts; it doubles as the catalog filter. The depths come from the sampling protocol, not from a designer.

2 · The reach

Where, and when — measured

The 218-cell grid from the release's grid table, with a cruise sweeping the core lines; and every dataset's years from the coverage the release measures at freeze time. Nothing asserted.

a cruise a season since1949
218 grid cells · 67 standard (quarterly since 1985) · 37 extended (winter, spring) · 114 historical — the 1950s pattern ran from 48°N to 20°N, the Strait of Juan de Fuca to Cabo San Lucas.
One row per dataset, one cell per year, shaded by how many casts or tows that year holds; the bars count datasets with data. Hatched rows are asserted (phytoplankton is region-pooled) or samples-only. Hover a cell.
Real: _data/grid.geojson (218 cells, the three patterns), _data/land.geojson, _data/datasets.json → coverage.years from observed_coverage() at v2026.09.06 Placeholder: the sweep order is a plausible zigzag, not a real cruise track

What it says without words

The grid is a shape people recognise; the fade from navy (core) to stone (historical) says the program once reached Oregon and Baja. The strip says 1949, the 1951 trio, the triennial 1970s, and that the CTD carries on where the bottle database pauses.

Honesty is the feature

The strip shows gaps. That is right: coverage is measured, never asserted is already the repo's rule, and the two hatched rows are the two datasets the rule exempts. A visitor who sees a gap has learned something true.

Where it points

A station click opens the Station Explorer on that cell; a strip cell opens the Explorer on that dataset and year; the odometer links to the 75th-anniversary timeline and calcofi.org/about/history.

Why a bento and not more bands

A band says "here is a category"; a tile says "here is a thing you can do". Six tiles of different sizes let the map and the years be large and the release and the log be small, which is their true weight.

What does not change

The catalog band and its includes; the product cards, their three-slug contract, the screenshots and the weekly brand check. The header gains one link (News) and keeps the lockup rule.

The one CTA

Brand v2 allows one yellow button per view. It stays in the hero — Explore the data — and every tile ends in a text link instead.

Where entries come from

  • release — generated from _data/versions.json (already on the site) and each version's RELEASE_NOTES.md heading.
  • dataset — generated from datasets.json's since_version: a dataset's first release is its news item.
  • app — an added: date on a product in products.yml; a "new" dot on its card and in the header for 30 days.
  • data · site · paper — hand-written rows in _data/news.yml (date, type, title, body, url).

Where it goes

A _plugins/news.rb beside datasets.rb merges and sorts; the tile shows four, /news/ shows all, feed.xml (jekyll-feed) lets calcofi.org's WordPress pull it into News & Updates, and the release notes page links back. Program news — cruises, people, papers — stays on calcofi.org and is linked, not copied.

A later tile

5 · Imagery

Field-guide plate, not cartoon

The direction, the palette, three prompts you can paste, and which engine to paste them into. And the route that needs no engine at all.

The direction

Flat vector with fine line-work, the way the 1950s CalCOFI atlas plates and Scripps' technical drawings did it: silhouettes with one interior line, a limited palette, light from the upper left, generous empty water. Scientific because the geometry is true; at ease because there is nothing to read inside the picture; exciting because it is one scene, not a diagram of boxes.

Keep from the proposal figure

  • The four beats — ship, harmonise, database, discover — as the story of the docs site, not the landing page.
  • The instinct to show instruments and species in one frame.
  • The habit of naming real standards (WoRMS, ITIS, NERC, EDI, OBIS) — in the page's text.

Drop

  • Gears, "AI" badges, padlocks, generic clip-art icons; every label inside the pixels.
  • Mixed rendering — glossy 3D beside flat vector beside a screenshot.
  • Boxes-with-arrows as the composition. One scene, one horizon, one palette.

The route with no engine

Part 1 is already it: the brand's 51-glyph sprite, forty lines of SVG for the ship and gear, and tokens for every colour. It themes, scales, links and animates today. Generated art is a layer behind it — a <picture> with a light and a dark source under the SVG overlay, pointer-events: none — so the page can ship now and gain the painted version when one is good enough.

Prompts

P1 · The hero section (raster, wide, two grounds)

Wide (21:9, 4K) scientific cross-section of the ocean off Southern California, drawn like an oceanographic section plot seen from the side: flat vector, field-guide plate, calm and precise.

Composition. The sea surface is a straight horizontal line one fifth from the top. Above it, a pale sand-white sky (#FFFFFF fading down to #E6EEF4). Below it, open water grading from pale cyan (#DCEDF5) at the surface through UCSD blue (#00629B) to deep navy (#182B49) at the bottom. The sea floor runs along the bottom: a deep basin on the left, one submarine bank rising midway, then a continental slope climbing on the right to a narrow shelf and a low headland that breaks the surface at the right edge, all in flat sand (#F5F0E6) with a thin stone outline (#B6B1A9).

The ship. One modern fisheries research vessel slightly left of centre, sitting on the surface line, as a crisp navy (#182B49) silhouette with a single white window strip: dark hull, blocky superstructure, a tall mast with a crossbar of sensors, an A-frame at the stern, steaming toward the coast on the right.

The gear, hanging to its true depth. A straight vertical wire from the ship's side down to a CTD rosette (a squat cylindrical frame of grey bottles) about a quarter of the way down the water; a diagonal wire trailing behind the stern at 45 degrees to a bongo net (two side-by-side rings with long fine conical nets) at a shallower depth; a short vertical wire to a small paired net just under the surface; a tiny rectangular net towed at the surface; a dotted intake line running along the surface behind the hull.

The life, at the depth it lives, each a simple two-tone silhouette with one interior line and no face: a chain of diatoms and one dinoflagellate just under the surface; a few copepods and a loose swarm of krill in mid-water; a scatter of fish eggs and one fish larva near the surface; a single lanternfish deep in the navy water; a blue whale at the surface ahead of the ship; three shearwaters in the sky.

Style rules. Flat vector, generous empty water, restrained. Palette limited to navy #182B49, blue #00629B, cyan #00C6D7, sand #F5F0E6, gold #C69214 and white. Gradients only in the water and the sky. No text, no labels, no arrows, no icons, no gears, no reflections, no lens flare, no 3D, no photorealism, no people.

Dark variant (same composition): sky #0F1A2E to #182B49, water #2A5A82 to #0B1524, ship silhouette #E9EDF3, sea floor #21375C with outline #34486B.

6 · Plan

Ship it in three cuts

Cut one needs no imagery and no new data source; it is the page you are looking at, in Jekyll. Cuts two and three add the painted layer and the live tiles.

Cut 1 · the structure · ~2 days
  • index.html: hero → numbers → bento → the existing catalog band → the product sections as a tab row.
  • assets/section.js: the section drawing from the brand sprite (part 1), pins from category.csv, links to calcofi.org methods.
  • assets/reach.js: the grid map and the years strip from _data/grid.geojson, land.geojson, datasets.json — all already on the site.
  • _data/news.yml + _plugins/news.rb + /news/ + feed.xml; added: on products.
  • The header keeps the contract; check_brand.py needs no change. Re-shoot the site's own card.
Cut 2 · the painted layer · ~1 day + review
  • P2 spots via Recraft (SVG) or GPT Image; drop them into the category tiles and the pins' hover cards.
  • P1 hero in two grounds via Nano Banana Pro; place under the SVG overlay as a <picture>; keep the overlay's pins and axes.
  • Screenshot both themes; the luminance check in scripts/shots.py catches a wrong ground.
Cut 3 · live tiles · later
  • At sea now from the cruise table and calcofi.org's cruise page.
  • Latest paper from calcofi.org/publications; next release from the pipeline's stamp.
  • The strip's hover opens the Explorer on that dataset and year; the map's click opens the Station Explorer.
