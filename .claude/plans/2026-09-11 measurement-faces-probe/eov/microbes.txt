GOOS Essential Ocean Variables (EOVs) are
defined as the minimum set of ocean
variables that are needed to assess ocean
state and variability for important global
ocean phenomena, and to provide essential
data for applications that support societal
benefit. They are derived from sustained
individual measurements, or combinations of
measurements, that can be undertaken at
global scale and in a cost-effective manner
(Martín Míguez & Heslop et al. 2026).

For each EOV, GOOS Expert Panels produce a
specification sheet, which serves as a guide
for understanding and contributing relevant
observations to support science and society.

These specification sheets are developed in
consultation with the ocean observing
communities to ensure that they are an
expression of community knowledge and
reflect   the  most     recent   monitoring
approaches and technology.

    3. GOOS Observing Specifications and Requirements
    This section sets out the measurements and observing design needed to produce comparable observations of this EOV that can be integrated across
    scales. It offers guidance for building a long-term, global system that captures key phenomena and delivers data fit for a range of uses. The guidance is
    not prescriptive, and your program does not need to meet every requirement to contribute. A global picture of this EOV emerges from the combined work
    of many observing efforts — local, regional, and global — and observations at any scale strengthen global ocean observation when shared openly.


    PHENOMENON: Changes or shifts in marine microbes distribution; Role in transport/cycling of elements
    EOV SUB-VARIABLE: Abundance (or Concentration) and/or Biomass
    Abundance or concentration: The abundance or concentration of microbial organisms in a sample, expressed as: cell abundance, gene copy number, cells
    per mL or g sediment, gene copy number per mL or g sediment from qPCR, absolute concentration of targeted organisms (CFU/mL or cells/mL from
    culturing. State which metric is being reported.

    Relative abundance: Proportion of sequenced taxa within a community (e.g. % of sequences)

    Biomass: The total mass of live microbial cells per unit (volume/area/mass of sediment — specify), estimated from cell size and volume measurements or
    from proxy measurements (total carbon or nitrogen in living cells). The estimation method and any conversion factors must be stated in the metadata.

    EOV SUB-VARIABLE: Classification9
    The taxonomic or functional classification of microbial organisms present in a sample, represented by the number of unique sequence variants
    (ASVs/OTUs), traits, or functions detected. Detection and/or identification can be achieved by sequencing10, (qPCR, amplicon for taxonomic classification;
    shotgun metagenomics for functional gene content and uncultivated genome; metatranscriptomics for in-situ activity), flow cytometry, or microscopy
    (specified in Section 3). Combined with microbial abundance or concentration, this sub-variable forms the basis for derived community composition,
    richness, and diversity products.
