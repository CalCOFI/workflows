What it is, how it is taken, why it matters

The pages under calcofi.io/measurements/ count a measurement faithfully and never show it. This study gives each one a face in three parts: what the thing is, drawn from its NERC concept down to the molecule; how CalCOFI takes it, from the bottle or the sensor to the reaction in the lab, linked to its method on calcofi.org the way the front door's pins are; and why it matters, on a scale a reader already knows and in the record's own 70 years.

The eleven measurements in the cast bar were chosen because each one breaks the design differently: an ion (nitrate), a gas (oxygen), a molecule of 65 heavy atoms (chlorophyll-a), a mixture (salinity), a pool of three species (dissolved inorganic carbon), a pure number (pH), a physical property (temperature), a living thing (Synechococcus), a key whose name disagrees with its chemistry (ammonium) and one with no concept at all (wind speed), and one that borrows a sibling's face and says so (estimated nitrate). Every structure was drawn from ChEBI's own coordinates today; every number is read from release or computed from it, with the computation named.

Recommendation

The page head, composed

The live head stays as it is: crumb, label, NERC ids, the stats band. Beneath it goes a face row in three columns, What · How · Why, and one sentence stitched from the NERC definition, the release record and an authored, cited line, each part underlined in the colour of its source. The other ways to say why (a second authored line, the GOOS question, the Wikipedia lead) wait one click away, collapsed the way the datasets catalog collapses what is not yet in the database. The structure is drawn in the page's own ink, so it is navy on white and bone on navy from the same file.

Part 1 · What

The thing itself

NERC's P01 concept is the key ring. It is not a label but a sentence built from parts, and the parts carry identifiers: a chemical object (S27) that is sameAs a ChEBI entry and a CAS number, a biological entity (S25) that is sameAs a WoRMS id, a property (S06) that is sameAs a QUDT quantity kind. Follow the chain to ChEBI and the structure comes with it, laid out by a chemist.

    Part 2 · How

    From the water to the number

    One card per series, because a measurement with two datasets is two methods: a Winkler titration and a membrane sensor are not the same act. Each card names the platform, the instrument, the principle in one or two sentences from the calcofi.org methods page and, where the analysis is a colour, the wavelength it is read at. The pin links to the section of the method page it came from, highlighted by a text fragment because the page has no stable anchors.

    Where in the water column

    Share of each series' values per depth band, from the record's own depth_bands. The bands are uneven on purpose (0–10, 10–50 … 2000+ m); the bar is the share, not a density.

    Part 3 · Why

    On a scale you know, and over 70 years

    Two figures carry the why. The first puts the measurement on a scale a reader already has, with the familiar things and the thresholds that matter beside the range CalCOFI actually sees; every mark has a source, and the scale turned out to be a quality check (the red marks). The second is the record's own history: each year's departure from the 1993–2013 normal for the same station, month and 10 m depth, from the release's climatology table, with the strong El Niño years shaded from NOAA's Oceanic Niño Index. It is drawn once per depth band the measurement is taken in, the same bands as Where in the water column, on one shared scale so the bands compare.

      How it would be built

      Two halves: the record computes, the fetcher borrows

      The measurement catalog's rule holds: the page reads the release record and nothing typed. What the release can compute (the anomaly series, the trend) goes into measurements.json; what CalCOFI authors (the method, the scale's references, the why and its citation) goes into four registries beside variable.csv and rides the release; what the world publishes (NERC, ChEBI, Wikipedia) is fetched weekly by the landing site, as the species faces are.

      1. metadata/measurement_*.csvfour registries with a source per row: _chem (ChEBI id and role), _method (dataset × type: platform, instrument, principle, nm, precision, reference, calcofi.org section), _scale (value, label, kind, source), _why (EOV, sentence, BibTeX keys, review status)
      2. build_measurements_catalog()schema 1.1: each key gains anomaly (band, yearly series, trend) from obs_env ⋈ climatology, and carries the four registries
      3. fetch_measurement_faces.pyweekly in the landing repo: NVS chain → ChEBI molfile → RDKit SVG in currentColor; Wikipedia leads; writes measurements_media.json to the public bucket
      4. _plugins/measurements.rbmerges both by key; _includes/measurement_face.html draws the row; the three sections draw from the mock's reference JS
      5. check_measurement_faces.pyevery scale mark has a source, every why a citation and a status, every structure a ChEBI id reached through NERC or a registry row

      • fixing now · 2026-09-11 CTD two-sensor averages fold in a dead sensor. On 36 casts (1998-07, 2007-11, 2024-01) the averaged salinity is half of sensor 1, about 17 against 34, and on 17 casts of 2003-04 the 1–2 m temperature reads 35–40 °C. Both are the source's average taking in a missing or out-of-range sensor (question Q21); every average is now recomputed from the valid sensors only.

      • filed to the CTD team CTD oxygen above 450 µmol/kg is a failed station correction on casts 2404_025 and 1907_050, where the station-corrected value is about 2.8 times the raw sensor.
        • 
          
      • provider question, not converted Wind speed may be in knots on some ships. NDBC buoy matchups (within 30 km, same 30-minute bin, 2016–2022, seven buoys) give median ship/buoy ratios of 1.7–2.4 on most 3322, 33UD and 39C2 cruises (knots-like) but 0.5–1.3 on most 33P4 cruises (m/s-like), confounded by true versus relative wind (METS Q22). The Beaufort face shows both readings until the provider answers.
        • 
          
      • fixing now · 2026-09-11 The key says ammonia; its chemistry says ammonium. The QC'd bottle series gets AMONZZXX (NH₄⁺), as its own pre-QC twin already has, and the label Ammonium.
        • 
          
      • fixing now · 2026-09-11 Loose and missing bounds. Nitrate, chlorophyll-a and phaeopigment declare no bound; the 2020-07-33P4 cast whose 99 °C was dropped still ships salinity 9.50.
        • 
          
      • fixing now · 2026-09-11 The METS keys have no category, so 17 live pages read "Measurement ·"; each gets the category of what it measures.
        • 
          
      • its own workstream The two catalogs do not meet. Synechococcus and Prochlorococcus carry WoRMS ids through NERC S25, but the picoplankton ingest emits the four flow-cytometry counts as obs_env rows with no taxon_key, and build_taxa_catalog() reads obs_bio only, so /species/worms-160572/ is a 404. A model change, planned rather than patched.
        • 
          
      • a design rule DIC's chemical object is "carbon atom" (NERC S27 → CHEBI_27594, roles include "antidepressant"): a pool of three species takes composition rows, never the S27, and ChEBI roles are never shown.
        • 
          
      • a design rule A05 is not the EOV membership test: EV_NUTS omits NTRAZZXX; the GOOS sheets' sub-variables are the list.
        • 
          
      • The record already tells the story. Oxygen has fallen in every band since 1984, faster with depth (−0.83 µmol/kg per decade at 0–10 m, −6.56 at 200–500 m) while nitrate rose at 50–200 m; the top 10 m ran +2.10 °C in 2015, its warmest year, though 1983 was warmer at 10–100 m; NOAA's ONI for June–August 2026 is +1.80, a strong El Niño under way while these pages are built.
